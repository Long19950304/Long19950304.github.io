China’s New-Energy Vehicle Exports Top Half of Exports.
China News Service reports that new-energy vehicles accounted for more than half of China’s vehicle exports for five consecutive months. The report, published on November 11, 2026, cites data from the China Association of Automobile Manufacturers for October, covering January through October for cumulative figures. Overall vehicle production reached 3.063 million units and sales 3.016 million units in October, rising 2.5% and 2% from September but falling 8.8% and 9.2% from a year earlier. From January through October, production totaled 26.318 million units and sales 26.286 million, both down about 5% year on year. New-energy vehicle production was 2.017 million units and sales 1.940 million in October, up 13.8% and 13.1% year on year. Their share of new-vehicle sales reached 64.3%. Total vehicle exports numbered 1.066 million units in October, up 1.3% month on month and 60.2% year on year. New-energy vehicle exports were 606,000 units, up 6.2% month on month and 1.4 times year on year, with their export share above 50% for the fifth consecutive month. The source does not provide a detailed product breakdown or explain which specific factors drove the changes. For research, users could compare the reported monthly and cumulative series without treating the export-share milestone as evidence about the broader market’s direction.

UK toy sales rise as hands-on products lead.
BBC News Business reports that UK toy sales increased 17% in the year to October, according to Circana, reaching a ten-year high for growth. The figures cover the same period in 2025 and 2026 and measure units of toys sold rather than sales value. Traditional games, squishies, stickers and building sets helped drive the increase. Circana UK toys director Melissa Symonds said connected toys represented less than 1% of market sales, describing traditional, hands-on play as overwhelmingly popular. The BBC also reported that toys priced between £5 and £10 showed significant growth. Separately, the value of sales to “kidults,” defined by Circana as toys and games for people aged over 12, rose 22% in the first half of 2026 compared with the first half of 2025. Toys for children remained the majority of sales. Another finding came from The Insights Family, which said one in five parents of children aged three to nine said opportunities for family play influenced their purchases. Nearly half of annual toy sales typically occur in the build-up to Christmas, so the November and December trading period remains important. The source does not provide a complete sales-value total, detailed product-category volumes, or separate monthly figures for October. The reported growth therefore describes the year to October, while the kidult result covers only January through June.

Google Introduces Gemini Decisions API.
Google AI announced the Gemini Decisions API, a new endpoint powered by Gemini Decision 1, available through the Gemini API in Google AI Studio. The vendor says it is designed for bounded choices, including tool selection, policy checks, model routing, and context grading. One multimodal input can combine text, images, audio, video, and PDFs, with up to 256K tokens, and can be evaluated against as many as 1,000 independent questions. The supported question types are yes/no checks, multiple choice, and ordered rubric scoring. Every answer includes a confidence score from 0 to 1, allowing applications to send ambiguous cases to a fallback or human reviewer. Google reports median model-compute latency of 50–100 milliseconds and end-to-end latency of 150–250 milliseconds, with latency described as nearly flat from 1 to 100 questions per request. It prices the model at $0.10 per 1M input tokens and $0 for output tokens, with a no-cost prototyping tier in Google AI Studio. The announcement also says the shared input is billed once per request, while each question adds instruction and criteria tokens. Google describes use in Gemini Notebook, Stitch, CC, and Search, and says integrations are rolling out across several developer platforms, with support in Google Cloud described as coming soon. These performance, latency, cost, and usage claims come from Google AI’s November 11, 2026 developer announcement; they are vendor-reported rather than independently verified here. As a practical research suggestion, teams could compare calibration and error rates on a small labeled dataset before using confidence thresholds in their own workflows.

Network-Inspired Metamaterials.
MIT News reported on November 11, 2026, that researchers from MIT, Northeastern University, and Utrecht University developed a network-inspired approach to designing metamaterials. Rather than repeating one uniform building block, their “scale-rich metamaterials” use interconnecting beams of different sizes, inspired by patterns in leaf veins and dragonfly wings.

The team used an algorithm to generate candidate beam networks, simulated their physical behavior, and then fabricated samples with 3D-printing methods that deposited polymer into the patterns. Each square sample was about the size of a credit card, with internal beams ranging from microns to centimeters. In physical tests reported by MIT News, researchers dropped a large weight onto samples and squeezed them with increasing force. Compared with conventional lattice and honeycomb metamaterials, the network-inspired samples were more resilient to compression and impacts, and the materials attained a widely controllable range of stiffness.

Study author Carlos Portela said that the range of beam sizes helps distribute damage and absorb energy, rather than allowing failure to become catastrophic. In another demonstration, the researchers used denser beam networks near the edges and showed that the material could cushion and protect a fragile object embedded at its center. MIT News says the procedure could support designs for impact-absorbing products such as helmets, packaging, and armor, as well as acoustic lenses; these are potential uses, not demonstrated commercial products. The evidence described comes from physical samples and specific laboratory tests, and the article does not establish long-term durability, manufacturing scalability, or performance in real products. The open-access Nature study was titled “Scale-Rich Network-Based Metamaterials.”

Injectable Hydrogel Sealant Tested for Gastrointestinal Fistulas.
The Chinese Academy of Sciences reports that researchers at its Institute of Process Engineering have developed an injectable hydrogel sealant inspired by slug mucus for gastrointestinal fistula repair. The approach combines endoscopic delivery with in-place sealing. According to the Academy’s November 11, 2026 account, the material passed through a 27G needle and a standard endoscopic channel while liquid at 25°C, then gelled within 30 seconds at 37°C. Its tissue-facing surface anchors through electrostatic attraction and physical entanglement, while its lumen-facing surface remains non-adhesive. The researchers call this asymmetric arrangement SET Janus adhesion. In laboratory tests, the adhesive surface had a shear strength of about 10.46kPa, compared with about 0.11kPa for the non-adhesive surface, and it sealed a 10mm ex vivo pig-stomach perforation without leakage. The Academy also reports about 98.6% inhibition of Escherichia coli and Staphylococcus aureus, together with favorable cell-toxicity, haemolysis, and long-term in-vivo safety evaluations. In a rat gastric fistula model, postoperative mortality was 3.3% for the hydrogel group, versus 6.7% for suturing and 56.7% for fibrin glue; no postoperative adhesions were observed in the hydrogel group. The study was published in Nature Biomedical Engineering. A clear limitation is that the work remains at the animal-level preclinical stage. The Academy says further research is needed on Ga3+ metabolism and clearance, pH effects on degradation, delivery systems, and scaled production; actual clinical efficacy requires further validation.
