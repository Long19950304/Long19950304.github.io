1. China Insurance Association Updates Commercial Health Insurance Guides

Xinhua Finance reports that China’s insurance association released model commercial medical-insurance clauses and its first recommended list for commercial health-insurance drug coverage on November 10, 2026. The association says the documents are non-mandatory reference texts intended to improve consistency in wording, claims materials and service standards.

China economy · 新华社财经 · 2026-11-10 · Original report

Read the full narration Xinhua Finance reports that the China Insurance Association released commercial medical-insurance model clauses and the first recommended list for commercial health-insurance drug coverage on November 10, 2026. According to the association, the materials are illustrative reference texts and are not mandatory. The release includes short-term and long-term general commercial medical-insurance model clauses, a model clause for city-specific commercial medical insurance, and the first drug-coverage recommendation list. The association says inconsistent policy wording, exclusions and claims-document requirements have contributed to differences among insurers. The model clauses standardize descriptions of medical institutions and 24 common terms, including deductible and medically reasonable and necessary. They also standardize selected statements on coverage and payment standards, exclusions, key reminders, claims documents and processing time limits. The association says the clauses promote allowing eligible emergency patients with sudden, critical or serious conditions to receive emergency care under broader access to contracted medical institutions where their coverage applies, within the terms’ protected scope. The recommended list applies to specified out-of-hospital drug and device coverage and accompanies the short-term and long-term general commercial medical-insurance model clauses. After several consultation rounds, the first selection contains 58 recommended drugs and devices. The association says indications cover cancers including lung cancer, liver cancer and blood cancers, as well as tumours included in China’s national rare-disease catalogue. It reports 25 category-one innovative drugs, two category-two modified new drugs and 32 self-paid drugs outside the basic medical-insurance catalogue. The list will be assessed as drug innovation and clinical use change. A suggested teaching point is to compare the clauses with actual policy wording, without assuming every insurer will adopt them or that coverage will be identical.

2. Fossil Reports a Return to Quarterly Profitability

CNBC Business reported that Fossil returned to profitability and sales growth in its fiscal third quarter. The company also raised its full-year sales outlook, although the guidance still implies a slight year-over-year decline.

World economy · CNBC Business · 2026-11-10 · Original report

Read the full narration CNBC Business reported on November 10, 2026, that Fossil Group’s fiscal third-quarter results offered early signs of a turnaround. For the quarter ended October 3, the company reported net sales of $271.2 million, up 0.4% year over year. It returned to operating profitability, recording $25.3 million in operating income, compared with an operating loss of $21.7 million a year earlier. Net income was $11.7 million, or 19 cents per share, versus a net loss of $39.9 million, or 76 cents per share, in the prior-year quarter. Operating expenses fell 11.1% to $137 million. Fossil raised its full-year net-sales outlook to between $980 million and $990 million, compared with previous guidance for a 3% to 5% decline. Even so, that range would represent a slight year-over-year decline from just over $1 billion in net sales in the prior year. The company expects positive free cash flow by year-end. Regional performance was mixed: Asian net sales grew 15.1%, with CEO Franco Fogliato attributing much of the growth to India, while direct-to-consumer sales fell 5.9%, which he attributed to aggressive downsizing. Traditional-watch sales grew 6.4%; smartwatch sales were under $1 million. The reported results cover one quarter and do not establish whether Fossil’s broader turnaround will continue. For research use, readers could compare subsequent quarterly sales, operating income, expenses, regional trends, and free cash flow against this October 3 quarter and the company’s revised annual guidance.

3. Mitra-v2 expands synthetic pretraining for tables

Amazon Science says Mitra-v2 uses larger and more varied synthetic pretraining tables for classification and regression. The company reports leading single-model results on TabArena, while noting that deployment includes brief table-level fine-tuning rather than pure zero-shot use.

Technology & research · Amazon Science · 2026-11-10 · Primary source

Read the full narration Amazon Science introduced Mitra-v2 on November 10, 2026, describing it as a tabular foundation model for classification and regression. The company says it was pretrained entirely on synthetic tables, without real or customer data, using a Transformer that attends across rows and columns. Compared with Mitra-v1, the pretraining tables have 10 times as many rows and roughly three times as many columns. A new hybrid causal-model generator combines neural, tree, Gaussian-process and autoregressive relationships within one table. By default, Mitra-v2 briefly fine-tunes on each new table and averages predictions from eight copies, so Amazon Science says it is not quite a zero-shot model. On the 51-dataset TabArena benchmark, the company reports that Mitra-v2 leads the single-model field, beating Mitra-v1 on 32 of 33 comparable datasets and completing all 51 within the one-hour budget. Fine-tuned Mitra-v2 also ranks first on ScoringBench. These results are publisher-reported and have not been independently verified here.

4. LISA’s Search for New Exoplanets

NASA reports that the developing LISA observatory could identify planets around tightly orbiting double white dwarf systems through features in their gravitational-wave signals. Researchers also describe a proposed use of the James Webb Space Telescope to confirm some of those detections.

Technology & research · NASA · 2026-11-10 · Primary source

Read the full narration NASA reports that two studies led by NASA astrophysicists show how the developing Laser Interferometer Space Antenna observatory could reveal a previously distinct population of exoplanets. The proposed targets are planets orbiting double white dwarf systems, which consist of two white dwarfs in tight orbits. The publication says LISA could detect a Jupiter-mass planet in such a system up to thousands of light-years away. This remains a prospective capability: the mission is still in development and is slated for launch in the mid-2030s. The detection idea works because a sufficiently massive planet causes the binary system to wobble. That motion adds a Doppler modulation to the nearly continuous gravitational-wave signal, producing secondary signals at higher and lower frequencies. NASA says these sidebands could reveal evidence of a planet and help determine which masses and orbital periods are most detectable. The first paper, authored by Casey McGrath, examines the detection method. A second paper by McGrath and coauthors investigates confirmation with NASA’s infrared-sensitive James Webb Space Telescope. NASA says Webb could detect infrared excess from a planet in a nearby double white dwarf system out to a distance of at least 500 light-years. In a test case at that distance, researchers found Webb could detect a planet about five times Jupiter’s mass, while LISA could detect a sub-Jupiter planet under ideal circumstances. Astronomers estimate that the Milky Way contains 10 million double white dwarf systems with orbital periods under one hour, and they expect LISA to resolve about 10,000 individual systems. A documented limitation is that LISA has not yet captured these exoplanet detections, while Webb’s confirmation ability applies within the study’s distance scenario.

5. Organic iron-sulfur framework aids photosynthetic carbon fixation

Chinese Academy of Sciences researchers report a synthetic material that combined independent iron-sulfur clusters with a stable framework and transferred electrons to isolated chloroplasts. In laboratory tests, the composite increased carbon-fixation activity over chloroplasts alone during 24 hours.

Technology & research · 中国科学院科研进展 · 2026-11-10 · Primary source

Read the full narration The Chinese Academy of Sciences reports that researchers from its Institute of Biophysics and Institute of Genetics and Developmental Biology designed a condensed organic iron-sulfur framework material, or OISF, containing independent iron-sulfur clusters. The aim was to combine the electron-transfer behaviour of biological iron-sulfur clusters with the stability of a condensed metal-organic framework. The team used a hydrothermal synthesis method and terephthalic acid as a linking ligand, producing a nanosheet framework rather than the dense lattice found in traditional iron sulfide minerals. According to the Academy’s research-progress account, characterization supported a structural link between condensed iron sulfide minerals and molecular-scale iron-sulfur clusters. The material showed redox activity, oxygen stability and electron-transfer capability, with its redox process adjustable under chemical conditions. Structural analysis associated electron transfer with dynamic formation of sulphur-sulphur bonds between independent clusters in different layers. The researchers then combined OISF with isolated chloroplasts. Their experiments found that carbon-fixation activity during 24 hours was 1.54 times that of chloroplasts alone, a laboratory comparison rather than evidence of improved crop yields. They also reported effective promotion of spinach growth after applying OISF to leaves, while describing its nanofertiliser potential. The main limitation is that the reported results come from experimental systems; broader agricultural performance and practical deployment were not established here. The findings were published in Small and received support from the National Natural Science Foundation of China and the Chinese Academy of Sciences.
