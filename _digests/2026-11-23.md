## 1. Salt Lake Lithium Project Reports Stable Output

China News Service reports that Qinghai Salt Lake Industry’s lithium project produced more than 40,000 tonnes of battery-grade lithium carbonate after entering production in September 2025. The company said the output could meet battery-material needs for about 800,000 new-energy vehicles, while process optimisation reduced resource consumption.

China News Service’s finance desk reported on 22 November 2026 that Qinghai Salt Lake Industry said its four-tonne-a-year lithium carbonate project had produced more than 40,000 tonnes of battery-grade material since entering production on 28 September 2025. The company said this volume could meet battery-material needs for about 800,000 new-energy vehicles. These are company-reported production figures and capacity-equivalence estimates, not independently audited results.

The report identifies a technical challenge at the Chaerhan Salt Lake, where the brine has a magnesium-to-lithium ratio exceeding 500 to one. Ma Shenggong, technical centre director at the company’s lithium technology branch, said the team developed an adsorption-based extraction method using a proprietary adsorbent to capture lithium ions from complex brine. According to the report, the project’s lithium carbonate has a main content of more than 99.7 percent and meets the national standard for Type I products.

Ma also said that, compared with the project’s initial design targets, annual electricity consumption fell by more than 40 percent, while brine and chemical auxiliary consumption fell by more than 20 percent. Freshwater and steam consumption recorded double-digit reductions. The report does not provide the underlying baselines, measurement period for these reductions, or an independent assessment, so the extent of the savings remains company-attributed. A brief teaching comparison could examine how extraction methods address differences in brine composition.

## 2. U.S. Pizza Chains Face Demand Pressure

CNBC reports that weaker discretionary spending, wider delivery choices, and changing eating preferences are pressuring major U.S. pizza chains. Sales slipped in 2025, though the evidence does not isolate how much each factor contributed.

CNBC Business reports that major United States pizza chains faced pressure in 2025 as consumers watched discretionary spending and chose among more delivery and takeout options. According to Technomic data cited by CNBC, sales at U.S. pizza restaurants fell 0.3% in 2025. Independent pizzerias recorded a 0.4% decline, while chain revenue, representing about two-thirds of domestic category sales, also fell 0.3%. These are annual year-on-year changes, and the figures were not labelled preliminary or revised.

Company results differed. Papa John’s reported North American same-store sales down 2.5% in 2025, and Pizza Hut’s U.S. same-store sales fell 5%. Among the three publicly traded pizza chains mentioned, only Domino’s reported annual growth at its U.S. restaurants, at 3%.

CNBC also cites a 2026 PwC survey reporting that spending at pizza quick-service restaurants was 22.2% lower per household after six to eight months of GLP-1 medication use. That survey finding describes an association within its data and does not establish that medication caused the overall chain sales decline.

The reported landscape includes nearly 17 dollars for an average large cheese pizza, lower-priced convenience-store and frozen alternatives, and more than 9,600 restaurant closures since the August 2019 peak. Circana’s David Portalatin estimated total pizza servings were down only about 1% in 2026, indicating the category remains substantial despite weaker restaurant sales.

## 3. Genetic lineages reshape pine conservation

The Chinese Academy of Sciences reports that transcriptome and DNA-marker analysis identified three highly divergent genetic lineages within the South China five-needle pine complex. Its model also flags groups in the western range margin, Hainan Island, and Da Lat as having higher future genomic vulnerability.

<p class="small text-muted">Technology &amp; research · 中国科学院科研进展 · 2026-11-23 · <a href="https://www.cas.cn/syky/202611/t20261118_5124531.shtml" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Primary source</a></p>

<details><summary>Read the full narration</summary>

The Chinese Academy of Sciences reports progress in conservation biology for a South China five-needle pine complex, covering the Pinus kwangtungensis complex. The research institute sequenced transcriptomes from 20 populations and 138 individuals across the complex’s distribution. The team also screened many genetic variant sites and used a distinctive set of mitochondrial DNA markers found in pine-family plants.

According to the Academy, these methods exposed evolutionary relationships, assessed genomic vulnerability, and helped establish conservation units. The study says the three named species previously recognized within the complex were not correctly delimited. Instead, the evidence supports three highly differentiated genetic lineages: EAST, WEST, and PDAL. The Academy says this divergence means the complex’s evolutionary diversity had been substantially underestimated.

The researchers also modelled genomic vulnerability under future climate scenarios. Their predictions indicated higher vulnerability among groups at the western edge of the distribution, on Hainan Island, and around Da Lat in Vietnam. As a suggested reading, these results make evolutionary lineages a more practical basis for conservation planning than the earlier named-species classification. The Academy recommends treating EAST and WEST as separate evolutionarily significant units, while treating the Hainan Island population of PDAL as one management unit. The report was published in Molecular Phylogenetics and Evolution. The source gives no confidence intervals for the vulnerability model, so the predicted risk areas remain model-based findings rather than measured outcomes.

## 4. Arctic Laser Station Advances Satellite Tracking

NASA reports that a faster, more precise satellite laser ranging system began operating at the Ny-Ålesund Geodetic Observatory in October 2026. The station is designed to strengthen the global tracking network and gather more observations of polar-orbiting satellites.

<p class="small text-muted">Technology &amp; research · NASA · 2026-11-23 · <a href="https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/laser-focus-at-the-top-of-the-world" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Primary source</a></p>

<details><summary>Read the full narration</summary>

NASA reports that a next-generation satellite laser ranging system officially began operating at the Ny-Ålesund Geodetic Observatory in Svalbard, Norway, on October 12, 2026. The Norwegian Mapping Authority operates the facility, a NASA partner, and NASA describes it as the first next-generation system of its kind in operation and the northernmost satellite laser ranging facility on Earth.

The technology sends laser pulses toward satellites carrying special reflectors and measures the time for each pulse to return. NASA says this approach helps determine satellite orbits, establish the terrestrial reference frame used to compare geographic measurements, and map subtle changes to Earth’s surface.

After development, testing, and calibration at NASA’s Goddard center, the new system has lasers capable of firing pulses thousands of times per second, compared with tens of times per second in older instruments. Its sensors can detect single photons, requiring less reflected light from a satellite.

NASA project manager Stephen Merkowitz said the far-north site improves the geographic distribution of the worldwide network and should provide more tracking opportunities for polar-orbiting satellites. From Ny-Ålesund, he said, these satellites can be observed almost every orbit, versus roughly two or three times daily from Goddard. The article reports that NASA is building two more next-generation systems, but it does not provide an independent performance comparison or quantified accuracy improvement. Its source and reference period are NASA Earth Observatory’s report, published November 23, 2026, covering the system’s October launch and operations through the article date.

## 5. A Smaller AI Model for Analog Chip Design

MIT News reports that a locally deployable small language model trained with SLIC can perform analog circuit design competitively with larger, resource-intensive models. The research suggests a route to faster, more private and customizable chip-design workflows, though reported results remain specific to the researchers’ tests.

<p class="small text-muted">Technology &amp; research · MIT News · 2026-11-23 · <a href="https://news.mit.edu/2026/ai-driven-technique-could-help-engineers-design-analog-chips-faster-and-less-expensive-1123" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Primary source</a></p>

<details><summary>Read the full narration</summary>

MIT News, in a report published November 23, 2026, describes a small language model designed to help engineers optimize analog integrated circuits. The method, called Small LM Iterating on Circuits, or SLIC, trains a model with a few billion parameters to work through circuit-design tasks using simulations and iterative refinement. MIT News reports that the model is inexpensive and efficient enough to run on a laptop and can be trained and deployed locally, allowing organizations to use their own circuit-design data without sending it to an external provider. In tests reported by MIT News, the SLIC model came close to matching the performance of GPT-6-Astra while using a fraction of the computational resources. The researchers also report that the model retained its performance on previously unseen circuit structures, more advanced technology nodes and real commercial processes. These are results from the research team’s evaluations, not an independently verified guarantee of performance in every design setting. The researchers describe several possible applications: reducing repetitive manual tuning, supporting faster prototyping and enabling more customized circuit development. They also note that the model produces natural-language explanations, which could help engineers understand its decisions, although MIT News does not provide a separate independent assessment of explanation quality. The paper’s authors include researchers from MIT, IBM Research and Analog Devices, and the work is funded in part by Analog Devices, IBM and the John L. Ocampo Endowment Fund of MACOM Technology Solutions. MIT News says the research is scheduled for presentation at the 2026 Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems. A practical research use would be to evaluate the method on one well-defined circuit task, compare it with an existing workflow, and measure design quality, computational use, time and review burden before drawing broader conclusions.
