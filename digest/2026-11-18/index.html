1. China Parcel Volume Exceeds 17 Billion for Two Months

China’s express-delivery development index rose 0.5% year on year to 477.8 in October, according to China News Service. The State Post Bureau’s report said parcel volume exceeded 17 billion for two consecutive months and forecast October revenue growth of about 3%.

China economy · 中国新闻网财经 · 2026-11-17 · Original report

Read the full narration China News Service reports that the State Post Bureau released figures for October, when China’s express-delivery development index reached 477.8, up 0.5% year on year. Parcel volume exceeded 17 billion for the second consecutive month. The report estimated that October parcel volume would rise slightly year on year, while express-delivery revenue would increase by about 3%. Sub-indices also recorded year-on-year gains: the development scale index rose 1.2%, the service quality index 0.1%, and the development capacity index 2.5%. The report described integrated production, sales and transport services supporting seasonal food distribution. It also said companies expanded services around airports, railway stations, scenic areas, hotels and speciality districts during the National Day holiday. These included appointment collection, luggage delivery, packing, consignment and on-site counter services. The State Post Bureau expected service capacity to increase further in November to meet peak-season delivery demand and anticipated stable operation. These are reported figures and forecasts; the October revenue estimate and November outlook were projections rather than final observed results.

2. Canada’s October Home Sales Rise Slightly

CBC Business reported that Canadian home sales increased 0.3 per cent month over month in October 2026, while remaining 10.6 per cent below October 2025. CREA also reported an average home price of $656,200, down 3.1 per cent year over year.

World economy · CBC Business · 2026-11-17 · Original report

Read the full narration CBC Business, citing the Canadian Real Estate Association, reported that Canadian home sales rose 0.3 per cent month over month in October 2026. That still placed sales 10.6 per cent below October 2025 levels. CREA senior economist Shaun Cathcart described little activity between September and October, noting that the result differed from some analysts’ expectations. The average home price was $656,200, down 3.1 per cent from 12 months earlier. At the end of October, 197,000 homes were listed through Canadian MLS platforms, slightly above the long-term average. Cathcart said Ontario and British Columbia remained slow, while prairie and East Coast markets were hotter than many people might realize. He expected national sales to remain relatively flat in the coming months as buyers waited for more certainty about interest rates. These figures describe October 2026 activity; they do not establish why sales moved or predict a later housing result.

3. Panzhihua Water Plant Launches Integrated Microgrid

China News Service reported that a microgrid combining hydropower, wind, solar, storage and direct current entered operation at the Bingcaogang Water Plant in Panzhihua. The project reportedly uses a 750-volt direct-current bus, can operate on- or off-grid, and has a reported supply reliability rate of 99.99%.

China economy · 中国新闻网财经 · 2026-11-17 · Original report

Read the full narration China News Service reported on November 17, 2026, that Sichuan’s first integrated grid-forming microgrid entered operation at the Bingcaogang Water Plant in Panzhihua. The system combines excess water-pressure generation, a 1,000-kilowatt distributed photovoltaic installation, a small wind generator, energy storage, and the plant’s pumping load. Its design uses a 750-volt direct-current bus to coordinate these components locally and reduce repeated alternating-current and direct-current conversion losses. The report says storage follows supply and demand, storing excess renewable electricity and releasing it when generation is insufficient. The project can switch between grid-connected and islanded modes, supports black start, and has a reported supply reliability rate of 99.99%. China News Service also reported that the installation includes an electromagnetic transient simulation model and an energy-management system intended for participation in electricity-market activities. However, the source provides no independent verification of the performance figures, and the reported rollout to other facilities remains prospective rather than confirmed.

4. Deep-learning model rebuilds gap-free sea surface temperature at 10 km scale

Researchers at the Chinese Academy of Sciences' Institute of Oceanology report a deep-learning framework that fuses infrared and microwave satellite readings to reconstruct sea surface temperature without cloud-related gaps. The study, published in the ISPRS Journal of Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing, reports better agreement with reference products than the operational MUR product, including for a 2023 typhoon cold wake.

Technology & research · 中国科学院科研进展 · 2026-11-17 · Primary source

Read the full narration The Chinese Academy of Sciences Institute of Oceanology reports new progress on gap-free sea surface temperature reconstruction, using a deep-learning framework the team calls remote sensing cross-scale reconstruction, or RSCR. The problem it addresses is familiar from satellite work: infrared sea surface temperature imagery is detailed but often blocked by clouds, while microwave readings see through cloud cover at coarser resolution. The team combines both, drawing fine structure from VIIRS infrared data and broad background constraints from AMSR2 microwave data, alongside a continuous seven-day time series to represent how temperatures evolve. The published paper describes a network built from positional encoding, a time-series encoder, a difference encoder, an attention fusion module and a multimodal decoder. Reported results say RSCR outperforms the operational MUR product across regions and seasons, with higher structural similarity and lower error. Power spectral density analysis indicates the model retains physically meaningful small-scale variance at short wavelengths, supporting ten-kilometre gap-free reconstruction rather than mere grid smoothing. As a case study, the team applied the model to the 2023 typhoon Doksuri, reporting that the recovered cooling in the cold wake core is closer to VIIRS reference observations and consistent with Argo float measurements. The work appeared in the ISPRS Journal of Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing, with support from national key research and development and natural science funding programmes. The source notes a limitation worth keeping in mind: evaluation depends on available reference observations, and cloud-free high-resolution truth remains difficult to obtain.

5. One-Atom Layer Reveals Interfacial Quantum State

University of Osaka researchers report directly observing two heavy-fermion states at an ultrathin ytterbium-copper layer on copper. Their work identifies electronic hybridization across the interface, while practical control and uses remain future research goals.

Technology & research · ScienceDaily · 2026-11-17 · Original report

Read the full narration ScienceDaily, reporting materials supplied by the University of Osaka, describes a 2026 study published in Communications Materials. Researchers created a single atomic layer of ytterbium and copper on a copper crystal and used synchrotron light to examine electrons at the boundary. The reported measurements identified two distinct heavy-fermion states. One was concentrated mainly within the two-dimensional atomic layer, while the other extended into the three-dimensional copper below. The team reports that this second state provided direct evidence of heavy-fermion behavior generated through interactions across the interface. According to the account, localized ytterbium electrons hybridized with mobile conduction electrons in the copper substrate, causing electrons to behave as though unusually heavy. Senior author Shin-ichi Kimura said the next goal is to engineer and control such states and investigate quantum states including unconventional superconductivity. The suggested reading is that engineered material boundaries may become a platform for unusual electronic behavior. That application remains prospective, not demonstrated. The study also faces the documented limitation that ScienceDaily says its supplied content may be edited for style and length, so the full journal article is needed for a complete methods and limitations review.
