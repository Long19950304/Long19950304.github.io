AI and China’s Smart Factories.
Xinhua Finance reported from Nanjing on 20 November 2026 that six departments had announced 34 factories in the second batch of China’s leading smart-factory cultivation programme. It said 49 such factories had been cultivated in total by that date. These are programme counts reported by Xinhua, not independently audited rankings.

At Nanjing Steel, Li Jinyan said more than 1,000 sensors around a blast furnace collected temperature, pressure, image, and other data in real time. According to Xinhua, the first-grade rate of molten iron increased by 10 percentage points. The report also said 217 company products and technologies had reached internationally leading levels, but it did not provide independent verification of that statement.

Xinhua quoted Zhou Ji, an academician of the Chinese Academy of Engineering, saying that combining next-generation artificial intelligence with advanced manufacturing could improve quality, efficiency, and reliability. Separately, Xiao Song said a Siemens shipbuilding project using AI and digital twins was expected to shorten construction time by 30% and increase production efficiency by 30%. These were vendor-attributed projections, not confirmed results, and the source gave no baseline period or measurement method.

For classroom analysis, students could compare sensor inputs with operational metrics and distinguish observed changes from projections. The source did not supply comparable data across factories, so broader performance conclusions remain limited.

U.S. Multinational Employment and Investment Rose in 2024.
The U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis reports that worldwide employment by U.S. multinational enterprises rose 0.9 percent in 2024, reaching 44.2 million workers. This compares with a revised 43.8 million in 2023, and the 2024 employment estimate is preliminary. Within that total, employment by U.S. parent companies increased 0.1 percent to 29.9 million, while employment by majority-owned foreign affiliates increased 2.7 percent to 14.3 million.

BEA also reports that worldwide current-dollar value added increased 8.0 percent to $7.4 trillion. Value added by U.S. parents increased 8.5 percent to $5.7 trillion, while foreign affiliate value added increased 6.5 percent to $1.8 trillion.

Worldwide spending on property, plant, and equipment increased 7.1 percent to $1.2 trillion. U.S. parents reported $968.7 billion, while foreign affiliates reported $216.2 billion. Worldwide research and development expenditures increased 10.1 percent to $622.7 billion. U.S. parents accounted for $536.5 billion and foreign affiliates for $86.2 billion.

A key limitation is that BEA revised its 2023 statistics using newly available and revised source data. The release also uses disclosure avoidance through rounding, aggregation, and ranges. BEA’s next scheduled release is August 20, 2027.

How a Wound Scent Switches Locusts from Recovery to Risk.
ScienceNet reports that a team from the Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences’ Shenzhen Institute of Agricultural Genomics published its study in Nature on November 19, 2026. The researchers report that injured migratory locusts release more guaiacol, a volatile compound produced by symbiotic microbes. In laboratory tests, applying guaiacol to healthy locusts caused other locusts to surround, bite and consume them.

The study’s central finding is a trade-off linked to injury severity. With small wounds, lower guaiacol release activated immune pathways associated with resistance to wound pathogens. With large wounds, release crossed a threshold and attracted healthy locusts that consumed the severely injured individual. The researchers therefore describe the compound as both a recovery signal and a warning of danger.

The team screened 82 candidate odor receptors and identified LmOR34 and LmOR36 as key components. Editing out either receptor removed attraction to guaiacol and stopped attacks on injured locusts, according to ScienceNet. The work combined chemical analysis, receptor testing, gene editing and behavioral observations.

These are laboratory findings reported by ScienceNet, not demonstrated field outcomes. The researchers also said that the mechanism may apply to other agricultural pests, but they identified unresolved questions about receptor interaction, signal integration and comparable effects in other insects. For teaching, the study offers a reproducible example of how one chemical signal can support recovery and increase risk depending on context.

Physics-Constrained AI Keeps Chaotic Models Bounded.
MIT News reported on November 20, 2026, that researchers from MIT’s Laboratory for Information and Decision Systems have developed a physics-constrained machine-learning method for chaotic dynamical systems. The aim is to prevent forecasts from drifting into physically impossible behavior over long periods, a problem the team describes as common in models of weather, climate, turbulent flows, ocean currents, and industrial processes.

The central mechanism is an energy-based constraint. According to the research team, the model learns an energy-like function and a boundary containing its chaotic behavior. A modular projection layer then adjusts predicted evolution so that, outside that boundary, the energy-like quantity must decrease. MIT News says this draws the simulated system toward the bounded region and establishes theoretical guarantees that predictions remain bounded over arbitrarily long forecast windows.

The team says this differs from earlier approaches that penalized violations during training. Such penalties do not guarantee compliance when a model receives new inputs, and an arbitrary numerical cap could block legitimate complex behavior.

In tests ranging from textbook cases to multidimensional systems, the constrained method closely matched true outcomes while conventional approaches developed impossible predictions, the team reported. The findings appeared in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, with open-source code available. The researchers plan to test the method on larger systems, including realistic weather models.

The documented limitation is that practical performance on large, realistic weather systems has not yet been established. The reported evidence covers controlled tests of physical systems, so broader usefulness remains a research goal rather than a demonstrated deployment result.

Researchers Advance Low-Cost Sodium-Ion Cathode.
The Chinese Academy of Sciences reports that researchers from the Dalian Institute of Chemical Physics and other teams advanced the design of a low-cost iron-manganese composite phosphate cathode for sodium-ion batteries. The work focused on understanding how intrinsic oxygen-vacancy defects affect the material during charge and discharge, according to the Academy’s account published on 20 November 2026.

According to the Academy, abundant oxygen vacancies destabilize local coordination in the crystal, create manganese/sodium antisite defects, obstruct sodium-ion reinsertion, and reduce reversible storage and stability. The researchers proposed a repair strategy combining orbital regulation with charge compensation. Under that approach, small amounts of suitable metal ions increase the formation energy of intrinsic oxygen vacancies while reducing antisite defects and improving structural stability and sodium diffusion.

The Academy reports that the resulting cathode raised first-cycle Coulombic efficiency from 87.0% to 97.0% and reversible specific capacity from 105.8mAh/g to 125.7mAh/g, compared with a theoretical specific capacity of 129mAh/g. Its cathode-based specific energy reached 421.1Wh/kg, versus about 350Wh/kg for composite sodium iron phosphate and about 380Wh/kg for sodium vanadium phosphate. In a pouch full cell paired with commercial hard-carbon anode, capacity retention was 93.6% after 1,000 cycles at a 1C rate. These are laboratory and pilot results reported by the Academy; broader commercial performance is not established here. The researchers also built a hundred-tonne pilot line that, according to the source, is fully connected and operating stably.
