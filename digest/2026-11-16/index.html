1. China Retail Growth Through October

China’s National Bureau of Statistics reported 1.0% year-on-year growth in total retail sales from January through October 2026, with October growth at 0.4%. Online goods and services grew faster, although expanded platform coverage limits comparison with the former online retail series.

China economy · 国家统计局 · 2026-11-16 · Original release

Read the full narration China’s National Bureau of Statistics reported total retail sales of consumer goods at 416,464 hundred million yuan from January through October 2026, up 1.0% year on year. In October alone, the total was 46,463 hundred million yuan, up 0.4% year on year. The agency says these figures are nominal growth rates, calculated without removing price effects. Excluding automobiles, retail sales totaled 383,811 hundred million yuan for the first ten months and grew 2.8%. October sales excluding automobiles reached 43,162 hundred million yuan, with growth of 2.5%. By location during January through October, urban retail sales were 359,918 hundred million yuan, up 0.9%, while rural retail sales were 56,546 hundred million yuan, up 2.2%. By consumption type, goods retail reached 369,261 hundred million yuan and catering revenue 47,203 hundred million yuan, with respective growth of 0.9% and 2.2%. Online goods and services reached 169,811 hundred million yuan, up 3.7%; online goods grew 2.9% and online services 5.0%. For a teaching exercise, students could compare the aggregate with major channels while noting that one total does not describe every segment. A documented limitation is that the agency expanded platform coverage and renamed the online-consumption indicator, stating that the new online goods and services series is not comparable with the former one. Another limitation is that some year-on-year calculations use adjusted comparison periods when survey coverage changes, so they may differ from previously published data.

2. In-Person Work and Office Design

NPR Business reports that economists Natalia Emanuel and Emma Harrington argue that working together in person can support creativity and collaboration. Their fall 2026 survey also indicates that simply requiring office attendance does not necessarily create meaningful face-to-face interaction.

World economy · NPR Business · 2026-11-14 · Original report

Read the full narration NPR Business reported on November 14, 2026, that economists Natalia Emanuel and Emma Harrington say working side by side can foster creativity, feedback, mentoring, and social connection. In their book, In Person, they argue that the accidental conversations available in shared workplaces may help colleagues exchange ideas and build relationships. NPR reports that Emanuel estimates nearly 1 out of 4 employees still works remotely, almost seven years after the pandemic began. She and Harrington also document a 20% drop in on-the-job feedback associated with remote work, along with less mentoring and greater isolation. However, the book’s central distinction is between presence and connection. In a survey conducted last fall, the economists found that nearly half of workers had no in-person meetings even when they were in the office, while 15% reported no face-to-face conversation with a coworker. These are the authors’ findings, as presented by NPR, rather than an independently verified assessment. For a practical reading, employers could examine whether schedules and shared spaces actually support informal interaction instead of assuming attendance alone will do so. Employers should also consider employee needs, because NPR notes that remote work has been a boon for parents, especially women. The evidence is limited to the authors’ arguments, their reported survey results, and NPR’s interviews; it does not establish how every workplace should balance flexibility, collaboration, or commuting.

3. Chinese researchers directly image non-equilibrium Thomson cooling

The Chinese Academy of Sciences reports that a research team directly imaged electron temperature, lattice temperature and thermoelectric response in HgTe and HgCdTe nanodevices. The study observed enhanced non-equilibrium Thomson cooling in a 1μm-wide HgTe channel, with reported compensation ratios of about 9% and 14.3%.

Technology & research · 中国科学院科研进展 · 2026-11-16 · Primary source

Read the full narration The Chinese Academy of Sciences reports research on direct imaging and enhancement of non-equilibrium Thomson cooling by teams from the Shanghai Institute of Technical Physics and other institutions. The source says the work simultaneously imaged electron temperature, lattice temperature and thermoelectric response in HgTe and HgCdTe nanodevice channels. It reports that the measurements directly observed a non-equilibrium Thomson cooling effect beyond the expectations of classical theory. According to the Academy’s November 16, 2026 account, the researchers prepared HgTe/HgCdTe quantum wells and combined two imaging techniques, SNoiM and SThM, to obtain spatial distributions under steady operation. In a constricted HgTe/CdTe device, the reported electron temperature reached about 1720K, producing a non-equilibrium Thomson compensation temperature difference of about 200K. A comparison HgCdTe device reached about 760K, with its thermoelectric signal nearly quenched. For a 1μm-wide HgTe channel, the source reports Thomson cooling compensation ratios of about 9% in the lattice channel and 14.3% in the electron channel. It contrasts these results with the roughly 2% efficiency limit described for the classical near-equilibrium regime. It also says that when the channel widened beyond 3.5μm, the response returned to the classical theoretical prediction. The Academy says first-principles calculations and a two-temperature model connected the response to temperature-dependent band effects and a Thomson-coefficient contribution. The findings were published in Nature Physics. A practical limitation is that the reported enhancement was confined to a narrow channel geometry; wider channels showed a different response. The study nevertheless offers a possible physical route for investigating nanoscale energy dissipation and on-chip heat management.

4. Stacked Mylar Insulation for Lunar Use

University of Pennsylvania researchers report that hundreds of stacked Mylar sheets can combine vacuum insulation with resistance to bending and compression. The early laboratory material is about one square centimetre, so scale-up and further durability testing remain necessary.

Technology & research · ScienceDaily · 2026-11-16 · Original report

Read the full narration ScienceDaily, reporting materials supplied by the University of Pennsylvania School of Engineering and Applied Science, describes research published in ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces in 2026. The team created hybrid layered insulating film stacks, called HyLIFS, by stacking hundreds of extremely thin Mylar sheets and applying an alumina coating to some samples. In vacuum tests, the best stacks had thermal conductivity comparable to advanced vacuum insulation materials while remaining flexible. Compression tests found that alumina-coated stacks could withstand the greatest stresses available from the laboratory equipment without losing their insulating performance. The researchers reported surviving 10 repeated cycles at approximately 20 megapascals. Models and measurements also indicated that microscopic antiblocking particles create contact points through which much heat passes between layers in a vacuum. The team further found that the thinnest characterised alumina-coated films performed similarly to medium-thickness films. This is a teaching point about material design: thickness alone does not determine thermal performance, because particle contacts and deformation also matter. The evidence remains preliminary. Samples were only about one square centimetre, and assembly required hours of careful manual work. Larger materials must still demonstrate equivalent insulation and mechanical strength, including resistance to repeated bending, impacts, scratches, and wear. ScienceDaily’s source is the University of Pennsylvania School of Engineering and Applied Science; the reference period is the research reported in 2026, with the article dated November 15, 2026.

5. ECNU Unveils Hyperspectral Satellite

ScienceNet reports that East China Normal University unveiled its first independently led hyperspectral satellite, Huashi-Kedaxing, on November 15, 2026. The university also launched the Liangtian Constellation plan, targeting a 200-satellite network around 2030.

Technology & research · 科学网新闻 · 2026-11-16 · Original report

Read the full narration ScienceNet, an established Chinese science newsroom, reported on November 16, 2026, that East China Normal University had unveiled its first independently developed low-orbit hyperspectral satellite at the university’s Putuo campus. Named Huashi-Kedaxing, the satellite is intended for space-based work. According to the report, the university and its teachers and students developed the payload, electronics hardware, and intelligent algorithms. The team designed, manufactured, and tested the hyperspectral camera and built a processing chain extending from onboard signal correction and high-dimensional data compression to quantitative retrieval on the ground. The university said the camera covers visible light, near-infrared, and short-wave infrared, with nanometre-scale spectral subdivision and metre-scale ground pixels per pixel. Its payload weighs less than 30 kilograms, according to the development team. Once launched, the lead satellite is planned to support research in smart agriculture and ecological monitoring, including crop-condition assessment and water-environment observation. The report also says the university plans, around 2030, to complete a 200-satellite network combining communications, remote sensing, and computing. Planned revisit intervals range from days to hours or even minutes. These constellation goals and in-orbit capabilities remain planned; the report confirms the satellite’s unveiling but does not provide a launch date or independently verified performance results.
