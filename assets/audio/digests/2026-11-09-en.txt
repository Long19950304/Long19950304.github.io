China’s CPI Rose 0.7% in October.
China’s National Bureau of Statistics reported that the country’s Consumer Price Index rose 0.7% year on year in October 2026. Prices were unchanged month on month. The first ten months together averaged 0.9% above the same period a year earlier. The report separates year-on-year changes from month-on-month changes, so the two rates should not be treated as equivalent. Consumer goods increased 0.8%, while services rose 0.7%. The monthly picture was flat overall: consumer goods declined 0.1%, services rose 0.1%, food fell 1.0%, and non-food prices rose 0.2%. Food, tobacco, alcohol, and dining out together declined 1.6% year on year, reducing the overall index by about 0.48 percentage points. Within that group, fresh vegetables fell 16.9%, fresh fruit 3.8%, livestock meat 2.7%, and pork 8.6%, while eggs rose 10.1%. Among the other seven major categories, six recorded year-on-year increases and one declined. Transport and communications led the reported increases at 5.5%, followed by healthcare at 2.4%. Housing prices fell 0.3%. A documented limitation is that China’s index used the 2025 base from January 2026. The agency said classification lists, representative specifications, survey outlets, and weights changed; its estimated average effect on monthly year-on-year indices was about 0.06 percentage points. The data come from the National Bureau of Statistics, published November 9, 2026, for October and January through October 2026.

Cosm expands immersive cinema venues while keeping ticket prices premium.
CNBC Business reported on 7 November 2026 that Cosm is expanding an immersive large-format cinema business built around shared-reality domes. The company has opened four theaters since 2024, near Los Angeles, Dallas, Atlanta and Detroit, while its Cleveland venue is only a plan for 2027.

The operating theaters use concave, 87-foot-diameter screens. In theatrical presentations, the film remains in a large central rectangle while visual environments extend across the surrounding dome. CNBC reported that Los Angeles and Dallas each accommodate around 350 seats; the newer Atlanta and Detroit domes have up to 500. Cosm has not disclosed Cleveland’s seat count, and its planned screen is 100 feet in diameter.

Cosm’s film program includes The Matrix, Willy Wonka & the Chocolate Factory and Harry Potter and the Sorcerer’s Stone. Shrek was scheduled to debut on December 16, described by CNBC as the company’s first animated theatrical offering.

Pricing varies by venue and showtime. CNBC reported that tickets range from $18 for a matinee to around $70 for prime seats or in-demand showings, excluding added concession charges and items from screening-themed menus. For comparison, EntTelligence data cited by CNBC put the average standard movie ticket at around $13 and average premium tickets above $18 each.

The distinction between operating and planned capacity matters when assessing the network: four venues are open, but the larger Cleveland site has not yet opened, and its final seating configuration remains undisclosed.

abcFISH enables single-molecule imaging of circular RNA.
The Chinese Academy of Sciences reports that a team at its Center for Excellence in Molecular Cell Science has developed abcFISH, a method for imaging circular RNA directly within cells and tissues. The source describes it as addressing a difficulty in locating and quantifying circular RNA in situ, rather than measuring it only after extraction. Circular RNA generally shares its sequence with a linear RNA produced from the same gene, apart from the back-splicing junction, which makes selective single-molecule detection challenging. According to the source, abcFISH uses rolling-circle amplification to detect that junction. Two probes bind on either side, and amplification begins only when they are adjacent at the junction. The researchers report high sensitivity, a high signal-to-noise ratio, and reduced cross-hybridization with linear parent transcripts. The method also supports multiplex detection and was applied to cells, mouse tissue, and formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded sections. It can distinguish different circular RNA isoforms from the same gene locus and quantify several circular RNAs in place, while recording their distribution across tissues, cell types, and subcellular regions. A practical teaching point is that junction-specific detection can help researchers explain why spatial information is valuable when biological molecules share most of their sequence. The source presents broader uses in functional analysis, biomarker exploration, and drug evaluation as prospects, not established outcomes. The research was published online in Nature Methods.

“乾阵”Interconnected Neutral-Atom Prototype Unveiled.
ScienceNet News, citing China News Service, reports that Qianyuan Quantum, based in Hefei, Anhui Province, unveiled the “Qianzhen” neutral-atom quantum computer prototype on November 8, 2026. The article quotes Li Gang, a professor at Shanxi University’s Institute of Optoelectronics and Qianyuan Quantum’s chairman, as saying that interconnection technology was incorporated from the prototype’s initial architecture design. He described it as China’s first neutral-atom quantum computer prototype with multi-machine interconnection capability. That status is a company representative’s characterization reported by the publisher, not an independently verified comparative assessment.

According to ScienceNet News, neutral-atom quantum computing uses optical tweezers to trap neutral atoms and construct qubits. The article lists strong scalability, high computational fidelity, and support for interconnected multiple machines as characteristics of this technical route, while providing no measured values. The report does not state the prototype’s qubit count, number of connected machines, task-completion time, fidelity results, comparison results, or deployment timeline.

A Shanxi University–Qianyuan Quantum joint laboratory was also inaugurated, with reported research areas including optical-tweezer atom arrays, multi-machine interconnection interfaces, and core optical components. Li said the team plans to use multi-machine clusters and conduct interconnection demonstrations for research and industry users. These plans remain prospective. For reproducible evaluation, later evidence would need to document the interconnection method, benchmark tasks, configuration, and measurement procedure.

Electrons Face a Space-Time Limit.
ScienceDaily, using material supplied by the University of Regensburg, reports an experimental discovery by researchers in Regensburg and Hamburg. Their 2026 Nature Photonics study used an ultrafast laser system and quantum simulations to examine electron motion. The team reports a fundamental “space-time limit”: an electron’s location and behavior over time cannot both be determined with arbitrary precision. In the experiments, timed light pulses transferred electrons from a sharp metal tip toward a silver surface across a few atomic diameters. Electrical-current measurements and changes in the pulse interval revealed electron motion on attosecond timescales. The electrons crossed an energy barrier through quantum tunneling. Simulations reproduced the observations and indicated that electron motion lagged the light field by approximately 500 attoseconds. The reported tradeoff is that more precise timing requires more energy, while the electron wave packet spreads more broadly in space. Even with substantial laser energy, the researchers reported enough localization for atomic spatial resolution and attosecond time resolution. The study appeared as “Tracking electrons at the space-time limit” in Nature Photonics, volume 20, issue 8, page 961. ScienceDaily describes possible uses in controlling chemical reactions and developing faster electronics and quantum information systems. The experiments demonstrated the constraint and measurement method, not those technologies. Electronics operating hundreds of thousands of times faster than dominant CMOS technology remains a long-term research goal, not an experimental performance result. For teaching, the case illustrates how a fundamental physical limit can define a boundary for later research.
