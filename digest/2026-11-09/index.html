1. China’s CPI Rose 0.7% in October

China’s National Bureau of Statistics reported that the Consumer Price Index rose 0.7% year on year in October 2026 and was unchanged from September. Food prices fell 2.9% year on year, while non-food prices rose 1.5%.

China economy · 国家统计局 · 2026-11-09 · Original release

Read the full narration China’s National Bureau of Statistics reported that the country’s Consumer Price Index rose 0.7% year on year in October 2026. Prices were unchanged month on month. The first ten months together averaged 0.9% above the same period a year earlier. The report separates year-on-year changes from month-on-month changes, so the two rates should not be treated as equivalent. Consumer goods increased 0.8%, while services rose 0.7%. The monthly picture was flat overall: consumer goods declined 0.1%, services rose 0.1%, food fell 1.0%, and non-food prices rose 0.2%. Food, tobacco, alcohol, and dining out together declined 1.6% year on year, reducing the overall index by about 0.48 percentage points. Within that group, fresh vegetables fell 16.9%, fresh fruit 3.8%, livestock meat 2.7%, and pork 8.6%, while eggs rose 10.1%. Among the other seven major categories, six recorded year-on-year increases and one declined. Transport and communications led the reported increases at 5.5%, followed by healthcare at 2.4%. Housing prices fell 0.3%. A documented limitation is that China’s index used the 2025 base from January 2026. The agency said classification lists, representative specifications, survey outlets, and weights changed; its estimated average effect on monthly year-on-year indices was about 0.06 percentage points. The data come from the National Bureau of Statistics, published November 9, 2026, for October and January through October 2026.

3. abcFISH enables single-molecule imaging of circular RNA

Researchers reported a new abcFISH method for imaging circular RNA in cells and tissues with single-molecule resolution and spatial information. The Chinese Academy of Sciences says the method supports multiplex detection and could broaden research using archived pathology samples.

Technology & research · 中国科学院科研进展 · 2026-11-09 · Primary source

Read the full narration The Chinese Academy of Sciences reports that a team at its Center for Excellence in Molecular Cell Science has developed abcFISH, a method for imaging circular RNA directly within cells and tissues. The source describes it as addressing a difficulty in locating and quantifying circular RNA in situ, rather than measuring it only after extraction. Circular RNA generally shares its sequence with a linear RNA produced from the same gene, apart from the back-splicing junction, which makes selective single-molecule detection challenging. According to the source, abcFISH uses rolling-circle amplification to detect that junction. Two probes bind on either side, and amplification begins only when they are adjacent at the junction. The researchers report high sensitivity, a high signal-to-noise ratio, and reduced cross-hybridization with linear parent transcripts. The method also supports multiplex detection and was applied to cells, mouse tissue, and formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded sections. It can distinguish different circular RNA isoforms from the same gene locus and quantify several circular RNAs in place, while recording their distribution across tissues, cell types, and subcellular regions. A practical teaching point is that junction-specific detection can help researchers explain why spatial information is valuable when biological molecules share most of their sequence. The source presents broader uses in functional analysis, biomarker exploration, and drug evaluation as prospects, not established outcomes. The research was published online in Nature Methods.

5. Electrons Face a Space-Time Limit

University of Regensburg researchers reported experimental evidence that electrons’ timing and spatial extent cannot be sharpened independently. The work demonstrates a fundamental research constraint; faster electronics and controllable chemical reactions remain long-term goals, not demonstrated outcomes.

Technology & research · ScienceDaily · 2026-11-07 · Original report

Read the full narration ScienceDaily, using material supplied by the University of Regensburg, reports an experimental discovery by researchers in Regensburg and Hamburg. Their 2026 Nature Photonics study used an ultrafast laser system and quantum simulations to examine electron motion. The team reports a fundamental “space-time limit”: an electron’s location and behavior over time cannot both be determined with arbitrary precision. In the experiments, timed light pulses transferred electrons from a sharp metal tip toward a silver surface across a few atomic diameters. Electrical-current measurements and changes in the pulse interval revealed electron motion on attosecond timescales. The electrons crossed an energy barrier through quantum tunneling. Simulations reproduced the observations and indicated that electron motion lagged the light field by approximately 500 attoseconds. The reported tradeoff is that more precise timing requires more energy, while the electron wave packet spreads more broadly in space. Even with substantial laser energy, the researchers reported enough localization for atomic spatial resolution and attosecond time resolution. The study appeared as “Tracking electrons at the space-time limit” in Nature Photonics, volume 20, issue 8, page 961. ScienceDaily describes possible uses in controlling chemical reactions and developing faster electronics and quantum information systems. The experiments demonstrated the constraint and measurement method, not those technologies. Electronics operating hundreds of thousands of times faster than dominant CMOS technology remains a long-term research goal, not an experimental performance result. For teaching, the case illustrates how a fundamental physical limit can define a boundary for later research.
