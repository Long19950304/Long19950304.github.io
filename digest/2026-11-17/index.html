1. China’s October Energy Output

China’s National Bureau of Statistics reported mixed October energy production: coal output fell more slowly, while crude oil and natural gas output grew. Electricity generation declined for the month, although wind and solar generation recorded faster growth.

China economy · 国家统计局 · 2026-11-16 · Original release

Read the full narration The National Bureau of Statistics reported China’s energy production for October 2026 in a release published on November 16. Its figures cover industrial enterprises with annual main business revenue of 20 million yuan or more, rather than every producer. In October, raw coal output was 380 million tonnes, down 5.8 percent year on year. That decline was 1.0 percentage point narrower than in September, with average daily output reaching 12.42 million tonnes. Crude oil production was 18.45 million tonnes, up 2.3 percent year on year, while daily output averaged 595,000 tonnes. Crude oil processing stood at 58.3 million tonnes, down 8.1 percent. Natural gas production reached 22.2 billion cubic metres, up 0.8 percent after a 1.0 percent decline in September. Overall electricity generation was 794 billion kilowatt hours, down 2.1 percent year on year, with average daily generation of 25.61 billion kilowatt hours. Thermal power fell 7.3 percent and hydropower 18.9 percent. Wind power rose 33.7 percent and solar generation 31.0 percent. From January through October, electricity generation was 8.2904 trillion kilowatt hours, up 1.8 percent year on year. NBS notes that annual changes in the enterprises included create differences from previously published figures, limiting direct comparisons. These are reported statistics, not projections.

2. US Bank Customers Report Service Disruptions

BBC News Business reported that customers at several major US banks experienced outages affecting online banking, ATMs, transfers and other services on Monday afternoon. By 18:00 EST, reported outages were declining, although the precise cause of the disruption had not been established.

World economy · BBC News Business · 2026-11-17 · Original report

Read the full narration BBC News Business reported that thousands of customers at major US banks, including JPMorgan Chase, Wells Fargo and Bank of America, reported difficulty accessing services on Monday, 16 November 2026. Problems began during the afternoon and included online banking, balances, transfers, ATMs and mobile applications. CBS News, which supplied additional reporting, described clients unable to view balances or complete bank and wire transfers. Chase also said some transactions were taking longer than usual, while customers still had access to their accounts and funds. Almost 5,000 reports of Chase service problems were recorded by Downdetector, although that figure represents user reports rather than a confirmed total of affected customers. Chase said a software bug in its hardware systems may have been responsible and described the issue as temporary, not systemic and not cyber-related. The exact nature of the bug remained unclear. Bank of America customers reported ATM and mobile banking problems. US Bank customers reported account, application and fund-transfer issues; Zelle error messages affected some payment attempts. Zelle said its service was operating as it should and that problems elsewhere could be responsible. By 18:00 EST, equivalent to 23:00 GMT, reported outages had fallen for Chase, Bank of America and US Bank, and BBC News Business said problems appeared to be easing for many customers by early Monday evening. The source also documents limitations: it does not provide a confirmed outage total, a detailed technical diagnosis or independent verification of the proposed cause. Truist, PNC and Capital One said they had not been affected.

3. Wireless Near-Infrared Switch for Neural Activity

The Chinese Academy of Sciences reported a wireless, near-infrared technique that switched neuronal excitation on and off in freely moving mice without implanted fibers or head-mounted devices. Mouse tests covered three brain regions and timescales from seconds to tens of minutes, but the evidence does not include outcomes in people or clinical applications.

Technology & research · 中国科学院科研进展 · 2026-11-17 · Primary source

Read the full narration The Chinese Academy of Sciences reported on November 17, 2026, that its Center for Excellence in Brain Science and Intelligent Technology has developed a wireless neural-control method called Dual-NIR Switch. In mouse experiments, the system used two near-infrared beams delivered through the skull to start and terminate neuronal excitation. The researchers said mice required neither implanted optical fibers nor a device on the head, and behavior could be adjusted while the animals moved freely. Control periods ranged from several seconds to several tens of minutes. The method combines near-infrared-responsive, dual-color orthogonal upconversion nanoparticles, called odUCNPs, with the photosensitive protein SOUL. The researchers reported that one brief 980-nanometer near-infrared exposure initiated neuronal excitation and allowed it to continue, while an 808-nanometer exposure reset the system. According to the Academy, illumination acted only at those two switching moments, reducing cumulative exposure and keeping photothermal risk within a safe range in the experiments. Tests covered three brain regions. Activity in the secondary motor cortex was controlled at seconds scale, including movement amount and circling behavior. The lateral hypothalamus was regulated at minutes scale to suppress feeding, while the ventral tegmental area supported conditional place-preference training lasting tens of minutes. The Academy described the results as advancing wireless optogenetics from continuous illumination to on-demand switching. A documented limitation is that the reported validation involved mice rather than people, so the findings do not establish clinical effectiveness or safety for human use.

4. Researchers report a more stable methanol-to-hydrogen catalyst

The Chinese Academy of Sciences reports that a molecular-sieve catalyst combined methanol steam reforming with high hydrogen output and low carbon monoxide levels in laboratory tests. The study also reports stable operation over more than 600 hours, while its broader industrial applicability remains untested.

Technology & research · 中国科学院科研进展 · 2026-11-17 · Primary source

Read the full narration The Chinese Academy of Sciences says a research team at its Institute of Process Engineering has developed a catalyst for producing hydrogen from methanol and steam. The design uses amino-functionalized Beta zeolite to anchor three-atom copper oxide clusters inside the pore structure, alongside nearby silanol nests that support hydrogen release. According to the Academy’s research account, this separates methanol activation from hydrogen production and helps protect the copper sites. In reported tests, the catalyst achieved nearly 97% methanol conversion at 275 degrees Celsius. At 280 degrees Celsius, its hydrogen production rate reached 951 molH2·molCu-1·h-1, while carbon monoxide by-product remained below 50 ppm. The team also reports more than 600 hours of cumulative stable operation across four regeneration cycles, and says performance exceeded that of copper nanoparticle and single-atom catalysts. The study combined in-situ infrared spectroscopy, hydrogen-deuterium exchange experiments and density-functional-theory calculations. The Academy says these methods indicated that methanol first dehydrogenates to adsorbed carbon monoxide, which then proceeds through a water-gas-shift reaction to carbon dioxide and hydrogen. One limitation is that the source reports laboratory catalyst performance rather than performance in a full commercial reforming system. It also presents possible extension to cobalt- and nickel-based catalysts as a research direction, not a demonstrated industrial result.

5. Open-weight models gain a unified serving route

Hugging Face says it has switched language-model inference to OpenRouter, allowing one token and API to access open-weight models across more than 80 providers. Its figures show sharply lower average prices than closed models, but the best open-weight systems still trail leading proprietary models on difficult benchmarks.

Technology & research · Hugging Face · 2026-11-16 · Primary source

Read the full narration Hugging Face announced on November 16, 2026, that language-model requests through its Inference Providers now run through OpenRouter. According to the publisher, OpenRouter routes requests across more than 80 providers using live measurements of price, speed and reliability, and retries on another provider if the first attempt fails. The existing code and Hugging Face token remain in use. The supported interfaces include OpenAI’s Chat Completions and Responses APIs and Anthropic’s Messages API. Hugging Face says every open-weight model on its Hub that a provider serves can be accessed through this route. For teaching and research workflows, users can choose a default routing policy, highest throughput, lowest price, or a specific provider. There is an important limitation: these are publisher-reported figures and service descriptions, not independent benchmark verification. OpenRouter’s share of tokens served by open-weight models reportedly rose from 26.8% in October 2025 to 71.7% at the end of September 2026, after peaking at 77.5% in July. Hugging Face reports average open-weight prices of $0.28 per million input tokens and $1.62 per million output tokens, versus $1.27 and $6.36 for closed models. However, the best open-weight model still trailed the best proprietary model on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, with a score of 46 against 65, despite costing about one-seventh as much per task. Pricing remains the provider’s price on the Hugging Face bill, with no Hugging Face markup. Image, video and audio generation continue directly through Inference Providers and do not use OpenRouter. A breaking change is that custom provider keys no longer work for affected language-model requests.
