## 3. ToyBlocks Offers Controlled Robot-Learning Tests

Hugging Face published a November 6, 2026 report introducing ToyBlocks, an open simulation framework for robot learning and evaluation. AllenAI reports reproducible experiments on data generation, model generalization, hierarchical planning, world models, and transfer from simulation to a physical robot.

<p class="small text-muted">Technology &amp; research · Hugging Face · 2026-11-06 · <a href="https://huggingface.co/blog/allenai/toyblocks" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Primary source</a></p>

<details><summary>Read the full narration</summary>

Hugging Face reported on November 6, 2026, that AllenAI introduced ToyBlocks, an open simulation framework for robot learning and evaluation. It combines a tabletop robot arm and blocks with automatic task and demonstration generation and behavioral metrics. According to the report, hundreds of task-and-motion plans can run in parallel on one graphics processing unit, reaching up to 24 times real-time performance. AllenAI’s initial spatial-generalization results for ManiFlow, π0.5 and MolmoAct 2 were poor despite large-scale pretraining, which the publisher presents as evidence that the problem remains unresolved. In another experiment, an agent using Claude Opus 5.5 solved or correctly declared tasks infeasible in 63% of cases; 50% of the scenes lacked required letters. The report says agent-written skill descriptions raised success from 24% to 63%, compared with 36% for a human-written description. For physical transfer, ManiFlow’s atomic-skill success was 80.0% in simulation and 81.1% in the real world. A hierarchical Molmo 2 plus ManiFlow system achieved 55.5% in 200 simulated episodes and 50.0% in 30 physical episodes. AllenAI said further trials are needed for statistical confidence.
