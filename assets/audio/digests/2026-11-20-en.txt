Port Tech and Faster China–Peru Trade.
China News Service, reporting on November 20, 2026, says Qianhai Port in Peru has expanded its digital operations while moving more containers and supporting trade with China. The article attributes operational gains to intelligent tools, but its evidence is descriptive and does not independently isolate their effect from port expansion, route growth or other factors.

The port began operations in November 2024, according to the publisher. It reports that shipping time between China and Peru fell from 35 to 40 days to 23 days. The Qianhai–Shanghai route is described as stable at 23 days, while three container main lines and five feeder services cover Asia, South America’s west coast, Central America and Panama.

Throughput rose from 336,200 standard containers in full-year 2025 to 340,000 standard containers in January–September 2026, a reported year-on-year increase of 47 percent. An intelligent vehicle-management platform began operating in September 2026, reportedly coordinating smart port vehicles through dynamic route planning. A ship-operations simulation system entered use in November, supporting pre-operation simulations and post-operation reviews, according to China News Service.

Trade figures come from two named Peruvian authorities. Peru’s foreign trade association reports 2025 bilateral trade at US$51.709 billion. It also reports Peru’s exports to China at US$31.309 billion in January–September 2026, up 31.3 percent year on year. Peru’s customs and tax authority reports cargo exported through the port at US$2.768 billion for that period, up 57.27 percent year on year. For teaching, students could compare throughput, shipping-time and trade indicators while noting that these figures cover different periods, measures and publishers.

Booking consolidates partner travel operations.
CNBC Business reported on November 18, 2026, that Booking Holdings is combining Booking.com Strategic Partnerships with Rocket Travel by Agoda in a new enterprise unit called Booking Partner Division. The operation is scheduled to begin in January, with Agoda CEO Omri Morgenshtern leading it. According to Booking, the division is intended to help banks, airlines, online travel companies and other businesses sell trips or create branded travel experiences for their customers.

The company said partners could use its travel supply and technology through white-label platforms, integrated applications, branded solutions and hotel-chain rate distribution. Booking reported that the portfolio contains more than 4.7 million properties, 33 million accommodation listings, more than 500 airlines and more than 200 car rental providers.

Morgenshtern told CNBC that combining product development and go-to-market work could help the business grow faster and serve partners better. He also said existing partners may receive new products, although Booking did not provide a timeline or financial estimates for that outcome.

The source also described Expedia Group’s enterprise business as a comparison. Expedia said in 2024 that its business-to-business revenue grew at a 16% compound annual rate from 2018 to 2023, while adjusted EBITDA grew at a 28% rate. For 2025, the company reported revenue of $4.8 billion, up 18%, and segment adjusted EBITDA of $1.26 billion, up 22%. These are company-reported figures for Expedia, not Booking results.

Booking expects to offer partners AI capabilities and is developing a travel-specific agent. However, Morgenshtern declined to say how much the new division will contribute to Booking’s overall revenue and profit. The reported $2 trillion market estimate is Booking’s estimate, not independently verified here, so the division’s financial impact remains unspecified.

Gene Identified for Tea’s Freshness Storage.
ScienceNet News, citing China Science Daily and reporting on research published on October 30 in The Plant Cell, describes a six-year project by teams from Anhui Agricultural University and Nanjing Agricultural University. The researchers identified CsVTT1 as a key gene involved in moving theanine, an amino acid associated with the fresh taste of tea, into plant-cell vacuoles, where it can be stored.

The article explains that tea trees synthesize and store substantial theanine in roots during autumn and winter, then transfer it to new shoots in spring. Accumulation in new shoots is often associated with a more pronounced fresh taste. The reported problem was identifying which membrane protein performs the transport, because several candidates can have overlapping functions and a short, dynamic transport process is difficult to observe directly.

The team used a screening, correlation, localization and functional-complementation sequence. These included a yeast mutant library, expression analysis linked to theanine levels, localization in root tissues, and transfer of the candidate gene into a heterologous system. The paper reports that introducing CsVTT1 restored growth in yeast lacking its corresponding gene. Researchers also directly observed theanine transport in African clawed frog oocytes, where efficiency was higher in an acidic vacuolar environment.

In tea hairy roots, increasing CsVTT1 activity raised theanine content by about 35%, while reducing it lowered content to less than 40% of the control. A cross-species test in rice increased arginine and seed protein content, according to the article. These results support a role for CsVTT1 in vacuolar nitrogen storage. However, the source presents breeding varieties, reducing fertilizer use and improving crops as future possibilities, not demonstrated outcomes.

Quantum Contextuality Becomes a Hardware Benchmark.
Google Research reported on November 19, 2026, that its superconducting Willow processor won the magic-square game 98.30% of the time, reported as 0.9830 ± 0.0001, versus a classical maximum of 88.89%. The team also reported χ = 5.590 ± 0.008 on a Bell–Kochen–Specker test, above the classical bound of 4. Contextuality-based games remained above classical bounds through 71 players, while a hidden-linear-function experiment used up to 105 qubits. Google says these tasks exposed coherent errors that random-circuit benchmarks may miss; results are specific to its hardware and setup.

Dry Ultrasound Material for Wearable Patches.
MIT News, in a report published November 19, 2026, describes research by engineers at MIT and the Singapore-MIT Alliance for Research and Technology on a dry material intended for wearable ultrasound patches. Conventional ultrasound uses gel to fill small gaps between a probe and the skin, helping sound waves travel efficiently. The researchers say hydrogel layers in earlier wearable designs can dry within a day or two, reducing their ability to transmit sound waves. Their liquid-free alternative uses an interpenetrating bottle-brush polymer structure, with two networks chemically fused to reinforce the material without adding liquid or lubricant. In a phantom model containing synthetic materials that mimic skin and internal structures, the team reports clear, continuous imaging for around nine days. A volunteer test during a 10-minute treadmill run found that conventional silicone rubber became water-logged with sweat, while the new material wicked away sweat buildup. In a separate field demonstration, a small patch about the size of a key fob was attached to a volunteer’s neck and connected to a portable tablet. MIT News reports that the device maintained continuous and accurate ultrasound imaging of blood flow throughout a 30-minute bike ride over steep inclines. The study is reported in the journal Science. The team is working to streamline fabrication, with the longer-term aim of enabling long-lasting wearable imagers for athlete performance and health monitoring outside clinical settings. A suggested research reading is that longer, stable imaging sessions may make wearable ultrasound easier to evaluate, although the reported evidence comes from laboratory, volunteer and synthetic-model tests. The documented limitation is that MIT News does not present the material as ready for clinical adoption, and further work is needed on fabrication and evaluation in broader use.
